For years,
Johnnie Walker has said one thing : Keep walking.

To keep moving forward.
And to pause, to find your own pace and notice where you are.
Both are part of the journey.

Hiking is a journey at your own pace.
Walking through open landscape, travellers come face to face with their own tastes, their temperament, and the way they live.

Scottish Highlands

The Scottish Highlands, stretching across the northwest of Scotland, are a region where vast natural landscapes, centuries-old whisky-making traditions, and a rich cultural heritage come together. With its rugged mountains and sweeping glens, the region has become a popular destination for hiking and road trips, attracting visitors from around the world.

This setting offers an opportunity to bring Johnnie Walker’s philosophy of “Keep Walking” to life through a journey of exploration and discovery. Walking through the Highlands and taking time to unwind amidst its natural beauty allows visitors to step away from everyday life, reconnect with themselves, and find inspiration for the journey ahead.

Inn, a home on the way

01 A Node

In eighteenth-century Britain, inns were the nodes that broke a journey into stages.
The Walker’s is the node between three trails, and it breaks the day into five stages.

02 A Home

An inn was built to make travellers feel at home: good food, good drink and a comfortable bed.
The Walker’s arranges all three around one goal — the best glass of the day.

Built from
the ground it stands on

We translated the Lewisian Gneiss strata
of the Northwest Highlands into architectural language.
The layered grain of the rock carries through the facade,
the circulation and the materials of every room.
Each day, guests walk one trail and end the day with a whisky matched to that path.
